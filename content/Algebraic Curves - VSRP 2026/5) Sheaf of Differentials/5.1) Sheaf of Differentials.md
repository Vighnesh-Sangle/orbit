### 5.1) Sheaf of Differentials

#### Algebraic Derivations

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 5.1.1**: (Derivations)
> Let $A$ be a $k$ algebra ($k$ need not be field). And $M$ an $A$ module. Then an $M$ valued derivation of $A$ over $k$ is a map $\delta: A\to M$ which is $k$-linear and satisfies $\delta(ab) = a\delta (b)+ b\delta (a) \fall a,b\in A$.
> \
> The set of all such maps is denoted by $\Der_k(A,M)$

We now construct an $A$ module $\Omega_{A/k}$, called the module of Kahler Differentials. There are two definitions, [[#^ee508d|Definition]] 5.1.2 and [[#^88141d|Definition]] 5.1.2', and there equivalence is [[#^c7e1fb|Proposition]] 5.1.3

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 5.1.2**: (Module of Kahler Differential $(\Omega_{A/k}, d)$)
> Let $F$ be a free $A$-module with basis $\set{da\ | \ a\in A}$ and let $N$ be the submodule generated by relations of the following type:
> $\set{d(a+b) - da - db \ | \ a, b \in A}$
> $\set{d(ca) - cda \ | \ a\in A, c\in k}$
> $\set{d(ab) - adb - bda \ | \ a,b\in A}$
> Let $\Omega_{A/k} = F/N$
> The map $d: A\to \Omega_{A/k}$ defined by $a\mapsto da$ is a $k$ derivation.

^ee508d

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 5.1.2**': (Sheaf of Kahler Differentials $(I/I^2, d')$)
> Let $\mu: A\otimes_k A\to A$ be the multiplication map, $a\otimes b \mapsto ab$
> Let $I$ be the kernel of this ring map. Then $I/I^2$ gets an $A$ module structure by $a.i = (a\otimes 1)i$ (which is same as $(1\otimes a)i$).
> $d': A\to I/I^2$ defined by $a \mapsto (a\otimes 1 - 1\otimes a)$ is a $k$-derivation

^88141d

> [!bluebox]+ **Proposition 5.1.3**: (Universal Property of Kahler Different)
> $(\Omega_{A/k}, d)$ satisfies the following universal property.
> Every $M$ valued $k$-derivation factors uniquely through $\Omega_{A,k}$ through $d$.
> $(I/I^2, d')$ also satisfies the same universal property.
> $(I/I^2, d') \simeq (\Omega_{A/k},d)$ with $(a\otimes 1 - 1\otimes a) \leftrightarrow da$

^c7e1fb

> [!bluebox] **Proposition 5.1.4**: (Eisenbud 16.2) (Relative Cotangent Sequence)
> If $R\to S\to T$ are maps of rings, then there is a right exact sequence of $T$ modules
>
> $$
> T\otimes \Omega_{S/R} \to \Omega_{T/R} \to \Omega_{T/S} \to 0
> $$
>
> where the right-hand map takes $dc$ to $dc$ and the left hand map takes $c\otimes db$ to $cdb$

> [!bluebox] **Proposition 5.1.5**: (Eisenbud 16.3) (Conormal Sequence)
> If $\pi:S\to T$ is an epimorphism of $R-algebra, with kernel $I$, then there is an exact sequence of $T\$ modules
>
> $$
> I/I^2 \to T\otimes_S\Omega_S/R\to \Omega_{T/R}\to 0
> $$
>
> where the right-hand map is given by $D\pi: c\otimes db \mapsto cdb$ and the left-hand map takes class of $f$ to $1\otimes df$

> [!bluebox]+ **Proposition 5.1.6**: (Eisenbud 16.9) (Commuting with Localization)
> If $S$ is an $R$ algebra and $U$ a multiplicative subset of $S$ then
>
> $$
> \Omega_{U\inv S/ R} \simeq U\inv S\otimes_S\Omega_{S/R}
> $$
>
> such that $d(1/s) = -ds/s^2$

^d0278e

> [!bluebox] **Proposition 5.1.7**: (Eisenbud 16.4) (Base Change)
> Suppose we have the $R$ algebras $R'$ and $S$. When we base change $S$ to $R'$, we get an $R'$-algebra $R'\otimes_RS$. Then
>
> $$
> (R'\otimes \Omega_{S/R},1\otimes d) \equiv (\Omega_{(R'\otimes_R})/R', d)
> $$

> [!greenbox]+ **Lemma 5.1.8**:
> If $K \subset L$, where $L$, $K$ are fields and $L/K$ is a separable extension. Then $\Omega_{L/K} = 0$

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> Let $a\in L$ and $f \in K[x]$ be its minimal polynomial.
> So, we have $f(a) = 0 \implies d(f(a)) = 0 \implies $f'(a)da = 0$But since$a$is separable,$f'(a) \neq 0$and thus we must have$da =0$This holds for all$a$and$\Omega\_{L/K}$is generated by these$da$, thus $\Omega\_{L/K} = 0\$

> [!greenbox]+ **Lemma 5.1.9**:
> Suppose we have map of rings $f: A\to B$ and a multiplicative set $S\subseteq A$ such that $f$ factors through $S\inv A$ making $B$ an $S\inv A$-algebra. Then there is an isomorphism  $\Omega_{B/A} \simeq \Omega _{B/S\inv A}$ that sends $da$ to $da$.

^8f4216

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> We will show $(\Omega_{B/A},d)$ satisfies the universal property of $\Omega_{B/S\inv A}$
> Let $M$ be a $B$ module and $\delta$ an $S\inv A$ derivation. Then $\delta$ is also an $A$-derivation and hence $\delta$ factors through a unique $B$-module morphism $\Omega_{B/A}\to M$. Further, $d: B\to \Omega_{B/A}$ is $S\inv A$ linear because it is an $A$-linear map between $S\inv A$ modules. Hence it is also an $S\inv A$-derivation.

#### Sheaf of Differentials

> [!proof]+ A description of the sheaf of Kahler Differentials:
> Given a scheme $X/Y$, we now construct a sheaf $\Omega_{X/Y}$ such that if we have affine opens $V = \Spec B$ in $X$ and $U = \Spec A$ in $Y$, such that  $V$ maps to $U$, that we get a map $A\to B$, then $\Omega_{X/Y}|_{V} \simeq \widetilde {\Omega_{B/A}}$.
> \
> Another way to describe this sheaf is via the universal property.
> Say we have a morphism of schemes $f: X\to Y$. This induces a morphism $\phi: f\inv \SO_Y \to \SO_X$, turning $\SO_X$ into a sheaf of $f\inv\SO_Y$-algebras.
> Now let $\SF$ be a quasi-coherent $\OX$-module. Then just as above, we may define a $f\inv\SO_Y$-derivation to be a map $\delta: \SO_X\to \SF$ such that each $\delta(U): \SO_X(U)\to \SF(U)$ is $f\inv\SO_Y(U)$-derivation.
> \
> Then there is a pair $(\Omega_{X/Y}, d: \SO_X\to \Omega_{X/Y})$ is a sheaf on $X$ and a $f\inv\SO_Y$-derivation with the universal property that every $f\inv\SO_Y$ derivation $\delta: \OX\to \SF$ factors uniquely through $d$, i.e., there is a unique $\OX$-module morphism $\varphi: \Omega_{X/Y} \to \SF$ such that $\delta = \varphi\circ d$

^c28fb4

We now construct a sheaf with the universal property as above:

> [!yellowbox] **Definition 5.1.10**: ($\Omega_{X/Y}$)
> Let $f: X\to Y$ be a morphism of schemes.
> \
> Let $\Delta: X\to X\times_YX$ be the diagonal morphism. Every point $x\in X$ has an affine open neighborhood $U$ that maps to an affine open $V$ in $Y$. $\Delta$ factors through $U\to U\times_VU$ which is a closed embedding since affine maps are separated. And $U\times_VU$ is an open subset of $X\times_YX$. Thus covering the image of $\Delta$ by such open sets, we get an open set $W \subseteq X\times_YX$ such that $\Delta: X\to W$ is a closed embedding.
> \
> The $\SI$ be the sheaf of ideals corresponding to this closed embedding. $\Omega_{X/Y}$ is defined to be $\Delta^*(\SI/\SI^2)$

^7f4af4

There is a lot of choice involved in the definition above. In the following, we show that any sheaf constructed as above satisfies a universal property and is thus unique upto unique isomorphism.

> [!greenbox]+ **Lemma 5.1.11**:
> Let $f: X\to Y$ be a morphism of schemes and $\Omega_{X/Y}$ be the sheaf of differentials. Then every point $x\in X$ has an affine open neighborhood $U = \Spec B$ that maps to an affine open $V= \Spec A$ such that $\Omega_{X/Y}|_{U} \simeq \widetilde {\Omega_{B/A}}$

^84d9d5

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> Take $U, V$ as in the construction of $\Omega_{X/Y}$ from [[#^7f4af4|Definition]] 5.1.10.
> The map $\Delta|_U: U\to U\times_VU$ corresponds to the ring map $B\otimes_AB\to B$ with kernel $I$.
> Then, $\SI|_{U\times_VU} \simeq \tilde I$ and $\SI/\SI^2|_{U\times_VU} \simeq \widetilde{I/I^2}$\
> \
> From this, we get that $\Omega_{X/Y}|_{U} \simeq \Delta|_U^*(\widetilde{I/I^2}) \simeq \widetilde{B \otimes_{B\otimes_AB} I/I^2}$
> \
> But note that $B \simeq B\otimes_AB/I$. So the above same as $I/I^2$ with $B$ action given by $b\cdot x \leftrightarrow b \cdot (1\otimes x) = b\otimes x = (b\otimes 1 \ \textnormal {mod} \ I) \otimes x \leftrightarrow (b\otimes 1)\cdot x$
> Thus $B\otimes_{B\otimes_AB}I/I^2$ as a $B$-module is isomorphic to $\Omega_{B/A}$ and we get $\Omega_{X/Y}|_U \simeq \widetilde {\Omega_{B/A}}$

> [!greenbox]+ **Lemma 5.1.12**: (Stalks of $\Omega_{X/Y}$)
> Let $f: X\to Y$ be a morphism of schemes and $\Omega_{X/Y}$ be the differentials. Let $x\in X$ be a point and $y = f(x)$. Then $(\Omega_{X/Y})_x = \Omega_{\SO_{X,x}/\SO_{Y,y}}$

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> From [[#^84d9d5|Lemma]] 5.1.11, we have open affine neighborhoods of $x$, such that $x\in \Spec B \subseteq X$ and $y\in \Spec A \subseteq Y$ and $\Omega_{X/Y}|_{\Spec B} \simeq \widetilde {\Omega_{B/A}}$. Suppose $x$ corresponds to prime ideal $p$ in $B$ and $y$ corresponds to $q$ in $A$.
> \
> Hence $(\Omega_{X/Y})_x \simeq (\Omega_{B/A})_p \simeq \Omega_{B_p/A} \simeq \Omega_{B_p/A_q}$, where the last second isomorphism comes from [[#^d0278e|Proposition]] 5.1.6 and the last isomorphism comes from [[#^8f4216|Lemma]] 5.1.9.

> [!greenbox]+ **Lemma 5.1.13**:
> Let $f: X\to Y$ be a morphism of schemes, $\Omega_{X/Y}$ sheaf of differentials. From [[#^84d9d5|Lemma]] 5.1.11, get $\set{U_i = \Spec B_i}$, a cover of $X$ by affine opens, and let $\set{V_i = \Spec A_i}$ be the corresponding affine opens $Y$.
> Then there exists an $f\inv\SO_Y$-derivation $d_{X/Y}: \OX \to \Omega_{X/Y}$ such that on each $U_i$, $d_{X/Y}|_{U_i}$ is induced be $d_{B_i/A_i}: B_i \to \Omega_{B_i/A_i}$, where $A_i = \SO_Y(V_i), B_i = \OX(U_i)$

^fedd6a

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> Let $\varphi_i: \OX|_{U_i} \to \Omega_{X/Y}|_{U_i}$ induced by $d_{B_i/A_i} : B_i \to \Omega_{B_i/A_i}$.
> The induced map on stalks is $\varphi_{i,x}: \OXx \to \Omega_{\OXx/\SO_{Y,f(x)}}$ is same as $d_{\OXx/\SO_{Y,f(x)}}$
> \
> So, $\varphi_i|_{U_i\cap U_j}$ and $\varphi_j|_{U_i\cap U_j}$ agree on stalks and thus are the same map. So, to collection $\set{\varphi_i}$ glues together to give a map $d_{X/Y}: \OX \to \Omega_{X/Y}$.
> \
> Now, to show that this is a morphism of $f\inv\SO_Y$-modules.
> The stalks of $f\inv\SO_Y$ are $(f\inv\SO_Y)_x = \SO_{Y,f(x)}$
> Looking at affine opens, $d|_{U_i}$ is induced by $d_{B_i/A_i}$, which is $A_i$ linear. So, if $x$ corresponds to $p$ in $A_i$ and $f(x) = q$, then  the $d_x$ is $A_{i,q}$ linear, i.e., $d_x$ is $\SO_{Y,f(x)}$ linear. And since this holds for all $x$, we get that $d$ is $f\inv\SO_Y$ linear.
> \
> Similarly, since it is a derivation on all stalks, it is a derivation on any open set and thus, $d$ is an $f\inv\SO_Y$-derivation.

Next, we show that the sheaf constructed above does satisfy the described universal property described [[#^c28fb4|here]].

> [!greenbox]+ **Proposition 5.1.14**:
> Let $f: X\to Y$ be a morphism of schemes. Then $(\Omega_{X/Y}, d)$ satisfies the following universal property:
> Given any quasi-coherent sheaf of $\OX$-modules $\SF$ and any $f\inv\SO_Y$ derivation $\delta: \OX\to \SF$, there exists a unique $\OX$-module homomorphism $\tilde\delta:\Omega_{X/Y}\to \SF$ such that $\delta = \tilde \delta \circ d_{X/Y}$

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> Let $\set{U_i}, \set{V_i}$ be open covers as in [[#^fedd6a|Lemma]] 5.1.13. Since $\SF$ is quasi-coherent, there are $B_i$ modules $M_i$ such that $\SF|_{U_i} \simeq \tilde {M_i}$.
> \
> We map $\delta$ induces an $f\inv\SO_Y(U_i)$-derivation $\delta_i:B_i\to M_i$. And we have map $A_i \to f\inv\SO_Y(U_i)$, turning $\delta_i$ into an $A_i$-derivation. Hence it factors through $(\Omega_{B_i/A_i}, d_{B_i/A_i})$. This will induce a map $\tilde\delta_i:\Omega_{X/Y}|_{U_i} \to \SF|_{U_i}$.
> \
> On stalks, $\tilde\delta_{i,x}: \Omega_{\OXx/\SO_{Y,f(x)}} \to \SF_x$ is the unique map induced by the derivation $\delta_x: \OXx\to \SF_x$. From this, we get that the $\tilde\delta_i$ glue together giving $\tilde\delta: \OX\to \SF$.
> \
> Finally, any other map $g$ that makes fits this description must have $g_x = \tilde\delta_{i,x}$, since it is the unique that map that we get from the universal property. Thus $g_x = \tilde\delta_x$ for all $x$ and so,  $g = \tilde \delta$, showing uniqueness.
