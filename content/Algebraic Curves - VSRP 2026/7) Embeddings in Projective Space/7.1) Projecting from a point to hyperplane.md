### 7.1) Projecting from a point to hyperplane

#### Ample and Very ample line bundles

The following are some simplified definitions of ample and very ample line bundles over varieties.

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition**: (Very Ample Line Bundle)
> Let $X$ be a variety of $k$ and $\SL$ a line bundle over $X$, then $\SL$ is said to be very ample if $\SL$ is globally generated and the map to $\PP^n_k$ defined by global sections of $\SL$ is a closed embedding.

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition**: (Ample Line Bundle)
> Let $X$ be a variety over $k$ and $\SL$ a line bundle over $X$. Then $\SL$ is said to be ample if $\SL^{\otimes n}$ is very ample for some $n$

> [!bluebox]+ **Proposition**: (Hartshorne IV 3.1)
> Let $D$ be a divisor on a complete non-singular curve $X$. Then
> a) The complete linear system has no base point if and only if for each $P\in X$, $\Dim |D- P| = \Dim |D| - 1$
> b) $D$ is very ample if and only if for any two points $P, Q$, $\Dim |D- P - Q| = \Dim |D| - 2$

> [!bluebox] **Proposition**: (Hartshorne IV 3.2)
> Let $D$ a divisor on a complete non-singular curve $X$.
> a) If $\deg D \geq  2g$, then $D$ does not have a base point.
> b) if $\deg D \geq 2g+1$, then $D$ is very ample

^166ee4

> [!bluebox] **Corollary**: (Hartshorne IV 3.3)
> Let $D$ be a divisor on a curve $X$. Then $D$ is ample if and only if $\deg D > 0$

#### Understanding the projection in terms of linear systems.

We know every curve can be embedded in $\PP^n_k$ for some $n$. To show that every curve can be embedded in $\PP^3_k$, we start with some $n$, take a point not a on curve and project onto a hyperplane such that map restricted to the curve embeds the curve in $\PP^{n-1}_k$.

To do this, we first understand the projection map $\PP^n-\set{P} \to \PP^{n-1}$ in terms of linear systems.

> [!greenbox]+ **Lemma**:
> Let $P$ be point in $\PP^n_k$ and $H$ a hyperplane not containing $P$. Then the projection map $\pi:\PP^n_k - \set P \to H \simeq \PP^{n-1}_k$ is given by the linear system of all the hyperplanes in $\PP^n_k$ passing through $P$.

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> The projection map is a dominant map of integral schemes. So the linear system corresponding to this map will simply be $\set{\pi^*D \ | \ D \textnormal{ a hyperplane in }\PP^{n-1}_k}$ because of the discussion [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/1) Setup/1.4) Maps to Projective Spaces and Linear Systems#Explicitly describing the pulled-back linear system|here]]. Let $A$ be a hyperplane in $P^{n-1}_k$ and let $A' =\pi\inv A$.
> \
> **Claim:** $\pi^*A = A'$
> The only codimension $1$ subscheme in $\pi\inv(A)$ is $A'$. And $A'$ maps surjectively onto $A$. Thus the generic point $\eta'$ of $A'$ maps to the generic point $\eta$ of $A$.
> \
> To get what the coefficient of $A'$ will be, after a change of coordinate and going to an affine cover, we may assume the $\pi$ is projection $\AA^{n} \to \AA^{n-1}$ onto the first $n-1$ coordinates and $A$ is the hyperplane $Z(x_1)$. Then $A'$ is also $Z(x_1)$. And the map is rings is inclusion $k[x_1,\dots,x_{n-1}]\to k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$. So, the induced maps on local rings sends $x_1$ to $x_1$ and thus coefficient of $A'$ is $1$.
> This finishes the proof of $\pi_*A = A'$
> \
> Finally, given any $B'$ a hyperplane passing through $P$, the $\pi(B')$ is a hyperplane B in $P^{n-1}_k$ such that $\pi^*B = B'$.
> \
> Thus the map $\pi$ is given by the linear system of hyperplanes passing through $P$.

> [!greenbox]+ **Lemma**:
> Let $X \subseteq \AA^n_k$ be an affine scheme of finite type over $k$, $P \in X$ and $f \in k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ such that $f(P) = 0$. Let $Y = Z(f)$. Then $T_P(X) \subseteq T_P(Y)$ inside $T_P(\AA^n_k)$ if and only if $\bar f \in\mathfrak m_{X,P}^2$.

^ecbff8

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> $T_P(V) = \Hom_k(\mathfrak m_{V,P}/ \mathfrak m_{V,P}^2, k)$ for $V = X, Y$ or $\AA^n$
> \
> For the forward implication, we have that there is an injective map $T_p(X) \to T_p(Y)$ that commutes their inclusions into $T_P(\AA^n)$
> Taking the dual, we get maps $\mathfrak m_{Y,P}/\mathfrak m_{Y,P}^2 \to \mathfrak m_{X,P}/\mathfrak m_{Y,P}^2$ that commutes with the natural projection maps from $\mathfrak m_{\AA^n,P}/\mathfrak m_{\AA^n,P}^2$
> Now we have $f\in\mathfrak m_{\AA^n,P}$, and $\bar f = 0 \in \mathfrak m_{Y,P}$. So, by the commutativity of the maps, $\bar f = 0 \in \mathfrak m_{X,P}/ \mathfrak m_{Y,P}^2$ and thus $\bar f \in \mathfrak m_{X,P}^2$.
> \
> For the backward implication, we construct a map $\mathfrak m_{Y,P}/\mathfrak m_{Y,P}^2 \to \mathfrak m_{X,P}/\mathfrak m_{Y,P}^2$ so that the diagram commutes and by taking duals, we get the required map $T_P(X) \to T_P(Y)$.
> Note that $\mathfrak m_{Y,P}/\mathfrak m_{Y,P}^2 = \mathfrak m_{\AA^n,P}/(\mathfrak m_{\AA^n,P}^2 + f)$.
> We have a map $\mathfrak m_{\AA^n,P} \to \mathfrak m_{Y,P} \to \mathfrak m_{Y,P}/\mathfrak m_{Y,P}^2$. It is clear that both $f$ and $\mathfrak m_{\AA^n, P}^2$ are in the kernel, thus inducing the required map.

> [!greenbox]+ **Proposition**: (Hartshorne IV 3.4)
> Let $X$ be a non-singular curve in $\PP^n$, $O$ a point not on $X$, and let $\varphi: X\to \PP^{n-1}$ be the morphism determined by projection from $O$. Then $\varphi$ is a closed immersion if and only if
> (1) $O$ is not on any secant line of $X$
> (2) $O$ is not on any tangent line of $X$

^6a2603

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> Let us look what the linear system on $X$ that defines $\varphi$ looks like.
> On $\PP^n - \set O$, the linear system $\mathfrak d$ defining the map is all the hyperplanes passing through  $O$. Let $(i, \phi): X\to \PP^n-\set O$ be the closed imbedding. Then the linear system on $X$ is same as $i^*(\mathfrak d)$.
> \
> $\phi$ is just restriction to the closed subvariety. So, given a prime divisor $W$ = $(f_i, U_i)$ on $\PP^n - \set O$, $i^*W = (\bar f_i, U_i\cap X)$. A point $P \in X \cap U_i$ is in the support of $i^*D$ $\iff$ $\bar f_i (P) = 0 \iff f_i(P) = 0$. And the zero set of $f_i$ on $U_i$ is exactly $W\cap U_i$.
> \
> So we get that $P$ is in the support of $i^*W$ if and only if $P \in W\cap X$.
> \
> By [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/1) Setup/1.4) Maps to Projective Spaces and Linear Systems#^ae5198|Proposition]], $\varphi$ is a closed embedding if and only if the $i^*\mathfrak d$ separates points and tangent vectors.
> \
> If $O$ on the secant joining points $P$ and $Q$, then all hyperplanes passing through $O$ and $P$ pass through $Q$ and vice verse. So the linear system does not separate points.
> \
> If $O$ is on the tangent to $X$ at $P$, then consider any hyperplane $W$ passing though $O$ and $P$. $W = (f_i, U_i)$ as a divisor. The tangent line is then contained in $W$. So, $T_P(X) \subseteq T_P(W)$ and by using [[#^ecbff8|lemma]] on an affine open containing $P$, we get $\bar f_i \in \mathfrak m_{X,P}^2$, and so $P$ has coefficient at least $2$ in $i^*W$. So $\varphi$ does not separate points.
> This proves the forward implication.
> \
> For the backward implication, given any two points $P,Q$, since the secant joining $P$ and $Q$ does not contain $O$, there is a hyperplane containing $P$ and not $Q$. Thus the linear system separated points.
> \
> And since $O$ is not on any secant, any hyperplane $W = (f_i, U_i)$ passing through $P$, $\bar f_i \in \mathfrak m_{X,P}- \mathfrak m_{X,P}^2$ by [[#^ecbff8|lemma]] again. So the coefficient of $i^*W$ is $1$ and the linear system separates tangent vectors. Thus $\varphi$ is a closed embedding.
