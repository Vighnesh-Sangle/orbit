### 7.2) Tangent Direction Map

#### Tangent space as the kernel of the Jacobian

Let $X \subseteq  \AA^n$ be an affine variety given by the ideal $(f_1,\dots, f_n)$.
For a point $P =(P_1,P_2,\dots,P_n)\in X$, the tangent space of $X$ at $P$, $T_P(X)$ is $\Hom_k(\frac {\fm_{X,P}} {\fm_{X,P}^2},k)$ which can be identified as a subset of $T_P(\AA^n) = \Hom_k(\frac {\fm_{\AA^n,P}}{\fm_{\AA^n,P}^2},k)$.

Let $J$ be the matrix given by $J_{ij} = \parde {f_i} {x_j} (P)$.
Note that $\frac {\fm_{\AA^n,P}}{\fm_{\AA^n,P}^2}$ has basis $(\bar x_i - P_i)$. From this we get a basis of $T_P(\AA^n)$, $\phi_j$ that sends $(\bar x_j - P_i)$ to $\delta_{ij}$. These $\phi_j$ in fact correspond to $\bar f \mapsto \parde {f} {x_j}(P)$ for $f \in \fm_{\AA^n,P}$.

We can think of $J$ as a linear map $\varphi: T_p(\AA^n) \to k^m$ where the chosen basis for $T_p(\AA^n)$ is $\parde {}{x_j}(P)$.

> [!greenbox]+ **Proposition**:
> With notation as [[#Tangent space as the kernel of the Jacobian|above]], $T_P(X) = \ker \varphi$

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> The map $\varphi$ is slot-wise equal to evaluating the vectors at $\bar f_i$.
> Suppose $v \in T_P(X)$ and $\pi: \frac {\fm_{\AA^n,P}}{\fm_{\AA^n,P}^2} \to \frac {\fm_{X,P}}{\fm_{X,P}^2}$.
> Then $v$ as an element of $T_P(\AA^n)$ is $v \circ \pi$. And evaluating this at $\bar f_i$, we get $v(\pi(\bar f_i)) =v(0) = 0$
> Thus $T_P(X) \subseteq \ker \varphi$
> \
> Now suppose $w \in \ker \varphi$. We need to show that $w$ factors through $\pi$.
> Note that $\fm_{X,P} = \fm_{\AA^n,P}/(f_1,\dots, f_m)$. So, $\frac {\fm_{X^n,P}}{\fm_{X^n,P}^2} = \frac {\fm_{\AA^n,P}}{\fm_{\AA^n,P}^2 + (f_1,\dots,f_m)}$. And so, we now know that the kernel of $\pi$ is functions that come from $\fm_{\AA^n,P}^2 + (f_1,\dots,f_m)$. To be precise, $\ker \pi = \frac {\fm_{\AA^n,P}^2+ (f_1,\dots,f_m)}{\fm_{\AA^n,P}^2}$
> \
> Since $w \in \ker \varphi$, $w(\bar f_i) = 0$ for all $i$. Thus $\ker \pi \subseteq \ker \varphi$ and so $\varphi$ factors through $\pi$.

#### The Tangent Direction map

Given a non-singular curve $C$ in $\AA^n$, to each point in $P$, we want to associate the direction of the tangent at that point, which will be a point in $\PP^{n-1}$ corresponding to the tangent line at $P$ shifted to the origin.

We define first define this set map, and then show that it is in fact a morphism $C \to \PP^{n-1}$

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition**: (Tangent Direction map $s: C\to \PP^{n-1}$)
> Let $C$ be a curve in $\AA^n$. Suppose $x_1,\dots,x_n$ are ordered coordinates on $\AA^n$. Then for every point in $\AA^n$, we have ordered basis $(\parde {}{x_1}(P), \dots, \parde {}{x_n}(P))$.
> \
> For any point $P \in  C$, $T_P(X)$ is a one-dimensional subspace of $T_P(\AA^n)$, generated by a vectors $(v_1,\dots,v_n)$ with respect to the basis above. Then the tangent line, i.e., the unique line through $P$ which corresponds to the same subspace of $T_P(\AA^n)$ as $T_P(C)$, is given by $P + \lambda(v_1,\dots,v_n)$ as $\lambda$ varies over $k$. So, the tangent direction is $(v_1:\dots:v_n)$
> \
> Define set map $s: C \to \PP^{n-1}$ by $P \mapsto (v_1: \dots: v_n)$ as above.

> [!greenbox]+ **Proposition**:
> With notation as [[#^8d4b1d|Definition]], $s$ is a morphism of varieties.

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> Suppose $C$ is given by the ideal $(f_1,\dots, f_m)$
> Consider that Jacobian matrix before evaluating. $J = [\parde {f_j} {x_i}]_{m\times n}$ as matrix in $M_n(k(x_1,\dots,x_n))$
> \
> Now fix a point $P\in C$, since $C$ is non-singular, let $J(P) = [\parde {f_j} {x_i}(p)]_{m\times n}$. The kernel of this matrix is the subspace $T_P(C) \subseteq T_P(\AA^n)$ with basis as in the definition of the tangent map.
> \
> The matrix $J(P)$ has rank $(n-1)$. So there is an $(n-1\times n-1)$ submatrix $M_P$ of $J(x)$ with non-zero determinant.
> Say this corresponds to rows $i_1 < i_2 <\dots< i_{n-1}$ and columns $j_1 < j_2 <\dots< j_{n-1}$. Say the omitted column is $j_0$.
> \
> Let $f_p(x)$ be the determinant of the corresponding submatrix of $J$.
> $J_p$ be the $(n-1\times n)$ matrix corresponding the rows $i_1$ to $i_{n-1}$
> \
> Since $f_P(P) \neq 0$, $f_P \neq 0$. So, let $U_P$ be the open subset of $C$ corresponding to points $Q$ with $f_P(Q) \neq 0$. These $U_P$ cover $C$.
> \
> Now we restrict our attention to one of the $U_p$
> \
> **Claim:** For any $Q \in U_P$, the matrices $J(Q)$ and $J_P(Q)$ have the same kernel.
> \
> Since $J_P(Q)$ is submatrix of $J(Q)$, any dependence relation in the columns of $J(Q)$ will remain a dependence relation in the columns of $J_P(Q)$. So, $\ker J(Q) \subseteq \ker J_P(Q)$
> \
> Also, $\det M_P(Q) \neq 0$ because $Q \in U_P$. So, $J_P(Q)$ has rank $n-1$ and its kernel is dimension $1$. Since $\ker J(Q)$ is dimension $1$ and is contained in the kernel of $J_P(Q)$, they must be equal.
> \
> Thanks to this claim, we may use $J_P$ to obtain our tangent direction map.
> \
> Observe that the inverse of $M_P$ is $\frac 1 {\det M_P} \textnormal {Adj}(M_P)$ and $\det M_P = f_P$ is invertible is $U_P$. So all entries of $M_P\inv$ are regular functions on $U_P$.
> Kernel of $M_P\inv J_P$ same as kernel of $J_P$.
> $M_P\inv J_P$ looks like this:
>
> $$
> \begin{array}{c|ccccccc}
>        & 1 & 2 & 3 & \dots & j_0  & \dots & n-2 & n-1 & n \\
> \hline
> i_1     & 1 & 0 & 0 & \dots & -a_1 & \dots & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
> i_2     & 0 & 1 & 0 & \dots & -a_2 & \dots & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
> i_3     & 0 & 0 & 1 & \dots & -a_3 & \dots & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
> \vdots  & \vdots &\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots   & \vdots & \vdots\\
> i_{n-1} & 0 & 0 & 0 & \dots & -a_{n-1} & \dots & 1 & 0 & 0 \\
> i_{n-2} & 0 & 0 & 0 & \dots & -a_{n-2} & \dots & 0 & 1 & 0 \\
> i_{n-1} & 0 & 0 & 0 & \dots & -a_{n-1} & \dots & 0 & 0 & 1 \\
> \end{array}
> $$
>
> Here, $a_i$ are regular functions on $U_P$.
> So the kernel of $M_P(Q)$ would be points of the form $(v_1,v_2,\dots,v_{j_0},\dots,v_n) = (a_1(Q)v_{j_0} \ , a_2(Q)v_{j_0} \ , \dots, v_{j_0}, \dots, a_{n-1}(q)v_{j_0})$ as $v_{j_0}$ varies over elements of $k$.
> \
> So $s(q) = (a_1(q): a_2(q):\dots:1:\dots:a_{n-1}(q))$ and from this we get that $s|_{U_p}$ is regular. Since this works for all $U_p$, $s$ is a regular map.
