### 7.3) Embedding Curves in $\PP^3$

#### Sketch of Embedding Curves into $\PP^3$

Our goal is to show that every non-singular curve $X$  can be embedded in $\PP^3$.
From [[#^166ee4|Proposition]], taking a divisor on $X$ of sufficiently high degree, we can embed it in $\PP^n$ for some $n$.

Now if we had a point in $\PP^n$ that did not lie on any tangent of secant of the $X$ embedded inside  $\PP^n$, then by [[#^6a2603|Proposition]], we can project from that point and get $X$ embedded into $\PP^{n-1}$.

So, in the next two subsections, we show that the points on all the secants and all tangents can be written as a finite union of locally closed subsets of dimension $3$ and $2$ respectively. Thus they cannot cover all of $\PP^n$ if $n \geq 4$ and we get $X$ embedded inside $\PP^3$.

#### Setup

Let $X$ be a non-singular curve in $\PP^n$. Without loss of generality, $X \cap D(x_0) \neq \emptyset$. Let $X \cap D(x_0) =X_0$,  which is a non-singular affine curve.

$D(x_0) \simeq A^n$ and under the isomorphism, lines in $\AA^n$ correspond to open subsets of lines in $\PP^n$.

So, it is enough to show that the collection of points on the secants and tangents of $X_0 \in \AA^n$ along with the secants and tangents corresponding to the finitely many points at infinity, i.e., $X \cap Z(x_0)$ form a proper subset of $U_0$.

#### Tangents from points at infinity

There are finitely many points, so tangents form a proper closed subset of $\PP^n$ and so of $D(x_0)$.

#### Tangents at points in $D(x_0)$

We can parametrize the points on the tangents in $D(x_0)$ by points of $X_0\times \AA^1$ which is a two dimensional space. But to say that the set of all points on the tangents are inside a two dimensional subset of $D(x_0)$, we would want this parametrization to be regular.

We make it regular by looking at open subsets of $X_0$ where we can continuously pick a representative for the tangent direction map and use it get a regular map.

We have the tangent direction map from  [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/7) Embeddings in Projective Space/7.2) Tangent Direction Map]], $s: X_0 \to \PP^{n-1}$.
Let $y_0,\dots, y_{n-1}$ denote the homogenous coordinates on $\PP^{n-1}$. Let $C_i = s\inv(D(y_i)$)

Embed each $D(y_i)$ inside $\AA^n$ by setting $\varphi_i: (a_0:\dots,a_{n-1}) \mapsto (\frac {a_0} {a_i},\dots,\frac {a_{n-1}} {a_i})$. Note the $i$-th coordinate under this map is always $1$.

We also have the map $l: \AA^n \times \AA^n \times \AA^1 \to \AA^n$ defined by $(a, b, t) \mapsto a + tb$ where $a, b \in \AA^n$ and $tb = (tb_1,\dots,tb_n)$. Clearly, the image of $\set{a,b} \times \AA^1$ under this map is the line passing through $a$ and $b$.

We now give a map $t_i: C_i \times \AA^1 \to \AA^n \simeq D(x_0)$ such that the image of $\set a \times \AA^n$ is the tangent passing through $a$. We do it by the following chain of maps.

$$
C_i \times \AA^1 \to C_i \times D(y_i) \times \AA^1 \to \AA^n\times \AA^n \times \AA^1 \to \AA^n
$$

The first map is $\id_{C_i} \times s \times \id_{\AA^1}$ followed by embedding $C_i$ and $D(y_i)$ in $\AA^n$ by its given embedding and $\varphi_i$ respectively. Finally we apply the line map.

It is clear image of $\set a \times \AA^1$ is the tangent and the all of the maps above are morphisms, so $C_i\times \AA^1 \to \AA^n$ is also a morphism.

But $C_i\times \AA^1$ is dimension $2$. So, by [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/Appendix/Scheme Theoretic Results#^34e12f|Proposition]], then tangents corresponding to points in $C_i$ lie inside a subset of dimension at most $2$. And this holds for all the $i$. The union of a finite number of dimension $2$ and dimension 1(corresponding to the points at infinity) closed subsets of $\AA^n$ will be dimension $2$.

Thus, all the tangents are contained in a dimension $2$ subset.

#### Secants from points at infinity.

If $x$ is a point at infinity. Then $x$ corresponds to a point in $\PP^{n-1}$ which parametrizes lines in $\PP^n$.

So, for each point in $x$ at infinity, fix an element of $x' \in \AA^n$ such that $x'$ corresponds to $x$ under $\AA^n \to \PP^{n-1}$. For any point $y \in D(x_0)$, the secant joining $x$ and $y$ is the set of point of the form $y + tx'$ as $t$ varies over $k$.

So, to get all the secants connecting points of $X_0$ and $x$, we can use the map $X_0 \times \AA^1 \to \AA^n$ where $(a, t) \mapsto a + tx'$. The closure of the image of this map will be at most dimension $2$.

Secants joining two points at infinity are completely contained in $Z(x_0)$ and thus don't bother us.

#### Secants joining points in $X_0$

For this we consider the map $X_0 \times X_0 \times \AA^1 \to \AA^n$ under which $(a,b,t) \mapsto (a + (b-a)t)$.
Given any pair of distinct points $(a, b)$, the image of $\set{(a,b)}\times \AA^1$ is the secant joining $a$ and $b$.

So, all the secants joining points in $X_0$ are contained in the image of this map.
But $X_0\times X_0\times \AA^1$ is a three dimensional variety, hence the closure of its image is at most dimension.

So, with all the results above, the set of all secants and tangents of $X$ in $D(x_0)$ are contained in a union of closed subsets of dimensions at most three. If $X \subseteq \PP^n$ for $n \geq 4$ then there is a point not on any secant or tangent and projection from that point embeds $X$ in $\PP^{n-1}$.
