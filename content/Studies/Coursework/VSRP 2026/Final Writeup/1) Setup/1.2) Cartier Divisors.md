### 1.2) Cartier Divisors

#### Definitions and First properties

Cartier Divisors generalize Weil Divisors to arbitrary schemes. I like to think of the locally principal description of a Weil Divisor as a motivation for defining Cartier Divisors.

Before defining what Cartier Divisors are, we define an analog of the field of rational functions for an arbitrary schemes.

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 1.2.1**: (Presheaf of total fractions $\tilde\SK$)
> Let $X$ be scheme. For each open set $U$, let
> $S(U) = \{f \in \OX(U)| f_x \in \OXx \textnormal{ is a non-zero divisor}\}$
> Let $\tilde\SK(U) = S(U)\inv\OX(U)$ with restriction maps coming from localization.

> [!greenbox]+ **Lemma 1.2.2**:
> Elements of $S(U)$ are non-zero divisors in $\OX(U)$. Further, If $U\subseteq X$ an affine open, then $S(U)$ is the set of all non-zero divisors of $\OX(U)$. So $\tilde\SK(U)$ is the ring of total fractions of $\OX(U)$

^91b71d

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> Suppose $f\in S(U)$ and $fg = 0$. then $f_xg_x = 0$ for all $x \in U$. But $f_x$ is a non-zero-divisor. So $g_x = 0$ for all $x\in U$. Hence $g = 0$. This shows that $f$ is a non-zero-divisor.
> \
> Now suppose $U$ is affine.
> $f\in \OX(U) = A$ is a non-zero divisor $\iff$ $\mu_f:A \to A$ multiplication by $f$ is injective $\iff$ the map in locally injective. But here it is locally injective since $f$ is a non-zero divisor in all stalks. Hence $f \in S(U) \iff f$ is a non-zero divisor.

> [!greenbox]+ **Lemma 1.2.3**:
> The presheaf $\tilde\SK$ is separated.

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> Suppose $U$ is covered by $\{U_i\}$.
> $\frac f s \in \tilde\SK(U)$ such that $\frac f s = 0 \in \tilde\SK(U_i)$ for all $i$
> $\frac f s = 0 \in \tilde\SK(U) \iff \frac f 1 = 0 \in\tilde\SK(U)$
> Since $\frac f s = 0 \in \tilde\SK(U_i)$, so is $\frac f 1$. So $\texists s_i\in S(U_i)$ such that $s_if = 0 \in \OX(U_i)$ but from [[#^91b71d|lemma 1.2.2]] $s_i$ is a non-zero-divisor and hence $f = 0 \in \OX(U_i)$. Since $U_i$ cover $U$, this tells us $f = 0$ and hence $\frac f s = 0 \in \tilde\SK(U)$

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 1.2.4**: (Sheaf of total fractions $\SK$)
> This is defined to be the sheafification of $\tilde\SK$

Since $\tilde\SK$ is a separated presheaf, the natural map $\tilde\SK\to \SK$ is injective on all open sets. And by [[#^91b71d|lemma]] 1.2.2, since we are only inverting non-zero divisors, $\OX$ injects into $\tilde\SK$.
Thus there is an inclusion $\OX\to\SK$.

Also note that if $X$ is an integral scheme, then $\tilde\SK = \SK =$ the constant sheaf $K(X)$.

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 1.2.5**: (Cartier Divisors)
> The inclusion $\OX\to\SK$ induces an inclusion of group of units and we have an exact sequence:
>
> $$
> 0\to \OX^\times\to \SK^\times\to \SK^\times/\OX^\times\to 0
> $$
>
> The global sections of the quotient sheaf, i.e. $\Gamma(X, \SK^\times/\OX^\times)$ is group of Cartier Divisors

Elements of $\SK(U)$ can be described be giving an open cover $\{U_i\}$ and sections $f_i \in \tilde\SK(U_i)$ such that $\{(f_i, U_i)\}$ forms a gluing family.

A Cartier Divisor can be given by similar collection $\{(\bar{f_i}, U_i)\}$ with $\bar f _i \in \SK^\times(U_i)/\OX^\times(U_i)$. Refining the cover, we may assume $\bar f_i \in \tilde\SK^\times(U_i)/\OX^\times(U_i)$. And the requirement that these form a gluing family tells us $\bar f_i|_{U_i\cap U_j} = \bar f_i|_{U_i\cap U_j} \implies f_i/f_j \in \OX^\times(U_i\cap U_j)$.

So, explicitly a Cartier divisor is can be described by giving a collection $\{(f_i, U_i)\}$ such that $f_i \in \SK(U_i)$ and $f_i/f_j \in \OX^\times(U_i\cap U_j)$

Two Cartier Divisors described this was equal if they agree on cross intersections.
So $\{(f_i,U_i)\}$ and $\{(g_j,V_j)\}$ correspond to the same Cartier divisor $\iff$ $\fall i,j$ $f_i/g_j \in \OX^\times(U_i\cap V_j)$

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 1.2.6**: (Principal Cartier Divisor)
> A Cartier Divisor is said to be principal if it is in the image $\SK^\times(X) \to \SK^\times/\OX^\times (X)$

In the explicit description, a Cartier divisor is principal $\iff$ it can be written be obtained from $(f,X)$ for some $f\in \SK(X)$

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 1.2.7**: (Cartier Class Group $\CaCl X$)
> $\{\textnormal{Cartier Divisors}\}/\{\textnormal{Principal Cartier Divisors}\}$

> [!greenbox]+ **Lemma 1.2.8**: (Sheaf of Cartier Divisor)
> For an open subset $U$ of $X$, $\SK_U = \SK_X|_U$ and $\Gamma(U,\SK_U^\times/\SO_U^\times) = \Gamma(U, \SK_X^\times/\OX^\times)$.
> So, $\SK_X^\times/\OX^\times$ is the sheaf of Cartier divisors on $X$

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> $\tilde\SK_U$ = $\tilde\SK_X|_U$ is clear since the definition of $\tilde\SK$ only looks inside $U$.
> And sheafification commutes with restriction, hence $\SK_U = \SK_X|_U$
> \
> Further, the exact sequence
>
> $$
> 0\to \OX^\times\to \SK^\times\to \SK_X^\times/\OX^\times\to 0
> $$
>
> restricts to an exact sequence
>
> $$
> 0\to \SO_U^\times\to \SK_U^\times\to (\SK_X^\times/\OX^\times)_U\to 0
> $$
>
> Hence we get that $\Gamma(U,\SK_U^\times/\SO_U^\times) = \Gamma(U, \SK_X^\times/\OX^\times)$

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition/Proposition 1.2.9**: (Effective Cartier Divisor)
> A Cartier divisor is said to be effective if for some descriptions of the form $\set{(f_i,U_i)}$, $f_i \in \OX(U_i) \fall i$.
> \
> If $\set{(g_j,V_j)}$ is any other description of the same divisor, $g_j \in \OX(V_j) \fall j$

> [!proof]- _Proof:_
> Suppose $\set{(g_j,V_j)}$ is another description. Fix a $j$.
> Only look at $i$ such that $U_i$ intersects $V_j$
> $g_j/f_i \in \OX^\times(U_i\cap V_j)$. Since $f_i \in \OX(U_i)$ we get $f_i|_{U_i\cap V_j} \in \OX(U_i\cap V_j)$ and hence $g_j|_{U_i\cap V_j} \in \OX(U_i\cap V_j)$. $\OX$ is a subsheaf of $\SK$, hence $g_j$, which will be the gluing of all these $g_j|_{U_i\cap V_j}$ is in $\OX(V_j)$

#### Weil and Cartier Divisors

> [!bluebox]+ **Proposition 1.2.10**: (Hartshorne II 6.11, continuing [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/1) Setup/1.1) Weil Divisors#^7fecc9|Proposition]] 1.2.15)
> For a Noetherian normal scheme, there is group isomorphism between the group of Cartier divisors and locally principal Weil Divisors.
> \
> Further, if the scheme is locally factorial, then all Weil divisors are locally principal and hence we have an isomorphism between Cartier Divisors and Weil Divisors.
> \
> This isomorphism sends principal divisors to principal divisors and effective divisors to effective divisors. Thus inducing an isomorphism between $\Cl X$ and $\CaCl X$

^a2f5ac

The mappings are:
A Cartier divisor $\set {(f_i, U_i)}$ is sent to a divisor $D$ such that for any prime divisor $Z$, its coefficient is $\nu_Z(f_i)$ for some $f_i$ where $U_i$ intersects $Z$. This mapping always exists, but need not be an inclusion if they hypotheses fail.

A Weil divisor $D$ with a locally principal description $\set{(f_i, U_i)}$ is sent to the Cartier divisor corresponding to the same description. The entire content of the proof, other than [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/1) Setup/1.1) Weil Divisors#^7fecc9|Proposition]] 1.2.15 is in checking that these mappings are well defined.

> [!greenbox]+ **Proposition 1.2.11**: (Sheaf of Weil and Cartier Divisors)
> The mapping above, in [[#^a2f5ac|Proposition]] 1.2.10, gives a morphism of sheaves from the sheaf of Cartier divisors to sheaf of Weil divisors.

^c0c771

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> We only need to check that the mapping commutes with restrictions.
> Start with $\set{(f_i,U_i)}$ a Cartier divisor on $U$. Suppose $V\subseteq U$. The restriction of the Cartier divisor to $V$ is $\set{(f_i,U_i\cap V)}$.
> \
> For any prime divisor $Z$ that intersects $V$, it will intersect one of the $U_i$, call that $U_Z$ with function $f_Z$. Let the corresponding prime divisor on $U$ be $\bar Z$.
> \
> Coefficient off $\bar Z$ in the Weil divisor corresponding to Cartier divisor would be $\nu_{\bar Z}(f_z) = \nu_Z(f_Z)$ which is the coefficient of $Z$.
> \
> The coefficient of other prime divisors don't matter.
> This shows that the map commutes with restrictions and thus we get a morphism of sheaves.

> [!greenbox]+ **Corollary 1.2.12**:
> If $X$ is a Noetherian normal separated locally factorial scheme, then the sheaf of Cartier Divisors is isomorphic to sheaf of Weil Divisors.

> [!proof] _Proof_: Follows from [[#^a2f5ac|Proposition]] 1.2.10 and [[#^c0c771|Proposition]] 1.2.11
