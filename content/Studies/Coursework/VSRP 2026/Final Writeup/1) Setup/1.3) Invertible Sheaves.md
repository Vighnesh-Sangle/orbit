### 1.3) Invertible sheaf/Line Bundle

#### Definitions and First Properties

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 1.3.1**: (Locally free sheaf)
> A sheaf of modules $\SF$ on a scheme $X$ is said to be locally free of rank $n$ if there exists a cover $\set {U_i}$ of $X$ such that $\SF|_{U_i}\simeq \OX^n$ as $\OX$-modules

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 1.3.2**: (Line Bundle/Invertible Sheaf)
> A Line bundle is a locally free sheaf of rank $1$.

> [!greenbox]+ **Proposition 1.3.3**: (Hartshorne II 6.12)
> Let $\SL$, $\mathscr M$ are invertible sheaves on a ringed space $X$, then $\SL \otimes \mathscr M$ and $\SHom_{\OX}(\SL,\OX)$ are invertible sheaves. Further, $\SL \otimes \SHom_{\OX}(\SL,\OX) \simeq \OX$

^949208

> [!proof]- _Proof:_
> We can a refinement of the open covers on which $\SL$ and $\mathscr M$ are free to assume that for cover $\set{U_i}$, both $\SL|_{U_i} \simeq \SO_{U_i} \simeq \mathscr M|_{U_i}$
> Now $(\SL \otimes \mathscr M) |_{U_i} \simeq \SL|_{U_i} \otimes \mathscr M|_{U_i} \simeq \SO_{U_i}$
> \
> Also, $\SHom_{\OX}(\SL,\OX)_|U_i \simeq \SHom_{\SO_{U_i}}(\SL|_{U_i},\SO_{U_i}) \simeq \SO_{U_i}$
> \
> Now we define a map $\SL \otimes \SHom_{\OX}(\SL,\OX) \to \OX$ by defining it on the presheaf that sends $U$ to $\SL(U)\otimes_{\OX(U)}\Hom_{\OX(U)}(\SL(U),\OX(U))$. We get a map from this to $\OX(U)$ by sending $a\otimes f$ to $f(a)$.
> \
> On open sets where $\SL(U)$ is free, this induces an isomorphism. Thus, this map of presheaves induces an isomorphism at stalks. And after sheafification, the stalks remain the same. Hence we get the required isomorphism.

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 1.3.4**: (Picard Group $\Pic X$)
> The Picard group is the set of isomorphism classes of invertible sheaves over a space $X$ with $\otimes$ as the group operation, i.e. $[\SL].[\mathscr M] = [\SL\otimes \mathscr M]$ and $[\OX]$ as the identity element.

Picard group is in fact a group because of [[#^949208|Proposition]] 1.3.3

#### Divisors and Invertible subsheaves of $\SK$

> [!greenbox]+ **Lemma 1.3.5**: (Locally describing a subsheaf of a sheaf)
> Suppose $\SF$ is a sheaf on a topological space $X$.
>
> 1. $\set{U_i}$ is a cover for $X$ and we are given sheaves $\SG_i$ on $U_i$ such that each $\SG_i$ is a subsheaf of $\SF|_{U_i}$ and $\SG_i|_{U_i\cap U_j} = \SG_j|_{U_i\cap U_j}$, then there exists a unique subsheaf $\SG$ of $\SF$ such that $\SG|_{U_i} = \SG_i$
> 2. Given another collection $\set{(\SH_i, V_i)}$ with its gluing $\SH$ as above, $\SH = \SG \iff \SG_i|_{U_i\cap V_j} = \SH_j|_{U_i\cap V_j}$

^99595a

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> Set $\SG(U) = \set{s\in \SF(U) \ | \ s|_{U\cap U_i} \in \SG_i{U\cap U_i}}$
> Details in [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/Appendix/Sheaf Theoretic Results#^46a917|Lemma]] SH.1

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition/Proposition 1.3.6**: ($\SL(D)$)
> For a Cartier divisor $D$ on $X$ we description $\set{(f_i, U_i)}$, we define a subsheaf of $\SK$ by defining it locally as in [[#^99595a|lemma]] 1.3.3 as $f_i\inv\SO_{U_i} \subseteq \SK|_{U_i}$

> [!proof]- _Proof:_
> The consistency conditions on the description of a Cartier Divisors ensure that the hypotheses of the first part of [[#^99595a|lemma]] 1.3.5 are satisfied, thus giving a subsheaf $\SL(D) \subseteq \SK$ and the second part of the lemma give us well-definedness.

> [!bluebox] **Proposition: 1.3.7** (Hartshorne II 6.13)
> Let $X$ be a scheme. Then:
>
> 1. For any Cartier divisor $D$ on $X$, $\SL(D)$ is an invertible sheaf and $D\to \SL(D)$ establishes a one-to-one correspondence between Cartier divisors and invertible subsheaves of $\SK$
> 2. $\SL(D_1- D_2)\simeq \SL(D_1)\otimes \SL(D_2)\inv$
> 3. $D_1\sim D_2 \iff \SL(D_1)\simeq\SL(D_2)$

> [!bluebox] **Corollary: 1.3.8** (Hartshorne II 6.14)
> $D\mapsto [\SL(D)]$ gives an injective group homomorphism from $\CaCl X$ to $\Pic X$

> [!bluebox] **Proposition: 1.3.9** (Hartshorne II 6.15)
> If $X$ is an integral scheme, then the homomorphism $\CaCl X \to \Pic X$ is an isomorphism, i.e. every invertible sheaf is isomorphic to a subsheaf of $\SK$

#### Ideal sheaves corresponding to effective divisors

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 1.3.10**: $\SI_D$
> For an effective Cartier Divisor $D$, let $\SI_D = \SL(-D)$

Note that $\SI_D$ is an ideal sheaf, i.e. a subsheaf of $\OX\subseteq \SK$. And it is locally free, hence quasi-coherent. Thus we can talk about the closed subscheme corresponding to the divisor $D$ given by the ideal sheaf $\SI_D$.

> [!yellowbox]+ Notation: The closed subscheme above is denoted by $Y_D$

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 1.3.11**: (Support of an effective Weil divisor $\Supp D$)
> Suppose $D = \Sigma_{i=1}^n n_{Z_i}Z_i$ with each $n_i \neq 0$, the $\Supp D = \bigcup_{i=1}^n Z_i$

> [!greenbox]+ **Proposition 1.3.12**:
> Suppose $X$ is a Noetherian normal separated scheme and $D$ is a Cartier divisor. Then $Y_D$ is supported exactly on the points of $\Supp D$, $D$ seen here as a Weil Divisor under the correspondence of [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/1) Setup/1.2) Cartier Divisors#^a2f5ac|Proposition]] 1.2.10

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> Fix a point $x \in X$. Under locally principal description, there is a neighborhood $U_x$ of $x$ such that $D|_{U_x} = (f)$. In $U_x$, $\SI_D$ is given by $f\SO_{U_x}$ and hence $\SI_{D,x} = f \OXx$.
> \
> So, $Y_D$ is supported at $x \iff \SI_{D,x} \subsetneq \OXx \iff f \notin \OXx^\times$
> \
> Now suppose $f \in \OXx^\times$. For every prime divisor $Z$ with generic point $\eta$ that contains $x$, we have a ring inclusion $\OXx\to \SO_{X,\eta}$. Thus $f$ is a unit in $\SO_{X,\eta}$ as well and hence coefficient of $Z$ is $0$. And if $f$ is not a unit in $\OXx$, then it is contained in some height 1 prime ideal of $\OXx$ which will correspond to a prime divisor.
> \
> So we get $f \notin \OXx^\times \iff x \in \Supp D$.
> Thus $Y_D$ is supported at $x$ $\iff$ $x \in \Supp D$
