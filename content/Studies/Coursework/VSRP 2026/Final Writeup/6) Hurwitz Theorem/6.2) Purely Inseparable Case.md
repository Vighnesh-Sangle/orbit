### 6.2) Purely Inseparable Case

#### Some Field Theory

In this section we establish some results about field extension in characteristic $p$ which will allow us to deal with the case where the map $f: X\to Y$ between non-singular curves is purely inseparable.

> [!greenbox]+ **Lemma 6.2.1**:
> Let $K$ be a field of characteristic $p > 0$ and let $\Omega$ be its algebraic closure. Let $K^{\frac 1 p} = \set{\alpha \in \Omega \ | \ \alpha^p \in K}$ and $K^p = \set{\alpha^p \ | \ \alpha \in K}$.
> \
> Then $K^{\frac 1 p} \simeq K \simeq K^p$ such that the following diagram commutes
>
> $$
> \begin{CD}
> K^{\frac 1 p}@>\sim>> K \\
> @AAA @AAA \\
> K @>\sim>> K^p
> \end{CD}
> $$
>
> Where the horizontal arrows are $p$-th power maps,  $x\mapsto x^p$
> Thus $[K^\frac 1 p : K] = [K:K^p]$

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> The $K \to K^p$ is a an isomorphism is clear.
> Given any $\alpha \in K$, the polynomial $x^p - \alpha$ has a root $\beta$ in the algebraic closure. So $\beta^p = \alpha$ and $\beta \in K^{\frac 1 p}$. Thus $K^{\frac 1 p} \to K$ is surjective and hence an isomorphism.

Our goal is to show that given a function field $K$ of transcendence degree $1$ over $k$, $[K^{\frac 1 p}: K]= p$.

> [!greenbox]+ **Proposition 6.2.2**:
> Let $K$ be a field of characteristic $p > 0$ and let $\Omega$ be its algebraic closure. Then for any $\alpha \in \Omega$,
>
> - $[K(\alpha): K] = [K^{\frac 1 p}(\alpha^{\frac 1 p}): K^{\frac 1 p}]$
> - $[K^{\frac 1 p}(\alpha^{\frac 1 p}):K(\alpha)] = [K^{\frac 1 p}: K]$

^0e5f22

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> Let $f \in K[t]$ be the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$. Suppose $f = \sum a_it^i$
> Consider $f' \in K^{\frac 1 p}[t]$ defined to be $f' = \sum a_i^{\frac 1 p} t^i$, where $a_i^{\frac 1 p}$ is the unique $p$-th root of $a_i$ in $K^{\frac 1 p}$.
> \
> Observe that $(f'(\alpha^{\frac 1 p}))^p = f(\alpha) = 0$. So $f'(\alpha^{\frac 1 p}) = 0$
> If $g$ is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha^{\frac 1 p}$ over $K^{\frac 1 p}$, then taking $(g(\alpha^{\frac 1 p}))^p$ gives us a polynomial relation of degree equal to $\deg g$ that $\alpha$ satisfies. Thus $\deg g \geq \deg f$. But $g | f'$ and $\deg f' = \deg f$. Thus $f'$ is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha^{\frac 1 p}$ over $K^{\frac 1 p}$.
> \
> And so, we get $[K(\alpha): K] = [K^{\frac 1 p}(\alpha^{\frac 1 p}): K^{\frac 1 p}]$
> \
> The second equality follows by looking at the towers.

> [!greenbox]+ **Proposition 6.2.3**:
> Let $k$ be a perfect field of characteristic $p > 0$ and $L$ a finitely generated extension of transcendence degree $r$, then $[L^{\frac 1 p}: L] = p^r$

^a413fa

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> $r = 0$ case is easy to see since $r = 0$ implies $K$ is algebraic over $k$, hence perfect, hence $L^{\frac 1 p}= L$.
> \
> Assume $r \geq 1$. Let $\set {x_1,\dots, x_r}$ be a transcendence basis.
> Let $K_0 = k(x_1,\dots,x_r)$. We know that $[K_0^{\frac 1 p}: K_0] = p^r$.
> \
> We proceed by induction on the number generators of $L$ over $K_0$.
> $L = K_0$ case is done above.
> \
> Assume that if $L$ is generated over $K_0$ by $n$ elements, then $[L^{\frac 1 p}: L] = p^r$.
> We now that if $L$ is generated by $n+1$ elements, then the same holds.
> \
> Suppose $L = K_0(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_{n+1})$. Let $K = K_0(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_{n})$, so $L = K(\alpha_{n+1})$
> Now, $L^{\frac 1 p} = K^{\frac 1 p}(\alpha_{n+1}^{\frac 1 p})$. Hence by [[#^0e5f22|Proposition]] 6.2.2 $[L^{\frac 1 p}: L] = [K^{\frac 1 p}: K]$ and by induction hypothesis, $[K^{\frac 1 p}: K] = p^r$.
> \
> Thus we get $[L^{\frac 1 p}: L] =p^r$ and we are done by induction.

#### Frobenius Morphism

First let's look at the Frobenius morphism.
For a scheme $X$ all whose local rings are characteristic $p > 0$, the Frobenius morphism is the $p^{\textnormal {th}}$ power map.

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition/Lemma 6.2.4**: (Frobenius morphism of Schemes)
> Let $X$ be a scheme all whose local rings are characteristic $p$, then there is a map $F: X\to X$ such that $F$ is identity as a set map and the induced map $\OX \to \OX$ is the $p$-th power map.

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> Let $U\subseteq  X$ be an affine open. Suppose $X = \Spec A$
> The number $p\in A$ is zero in all localizations, hence $p = 0$ in $A$ and $A$ is a characteristic $p$ ring.
> \
> Let $F_U: U \to U$ be the map induced by the $p$-th power map $f_U: A \to A$, $a\mapsto a^p$
> Let $\mathfrak p$ be any prime in $A$. Then $a \in f_U\inv (\mathfrak p) \implies a^p \in \mathfrak p \implies a \in \mathfrak p$. And it is clear that $f\mathfrak (\mathfrak p) \subseteq \mathfrak p$. So we get $F_U(x) = x$ for all $x\in U$.
> \
> The map on local rings, $\OXx \to \OXx$ is also the $p$-th power map.
> \
> Since such affine $U$ cover $X$, to get a map $X\to X$, it is enough to show that the $F_U$s defined above glue. They clearly glue as set maps to get a map $F$. We want a map $\OX \to F_*\OX = \OX$. And here again, the maps $F_U$ induce same map on the stalks, so they agree and we get a morphism of schemes $F: X\to X$.

Note that $X$ is a variety over $k$, then $F$ is NOT a morphism over $k$. But the following diagram commutes.

$$
\begin{CD}
X @>F>> X \\
@VVV @VVV \\
\Spec k @>F>> \Spec k
\end{CD}
$$

From this we get the definition of $X_p$

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 6.2.5**:
> Let $f: X\to \Spec k$ be scheme over $\Spec k$. Then $X_p$ is same as the scheme $X$, but with the structure map $X \to \Spec k$ given by $F\circ f$ as in the diagram above.

Now $F: X_p \to X$ is a morphism of schemes over $k$.

#### Frobenius Morphism for curves

If $X$ is a complete non-singular curve, then $F: X_p \to X$ induces the $p$-th power map on the function fields. If $K$ is the function field on $X$. Then since $X$ and $X_p$ are isomorphic as abstract schemes, the function field of $X_p$ is also isomorphic to $K$ as abstract field. But as field over $k$, it is $K^\frac 1 p$.

The $F$ corresponds to $K \to K^\frac 1 p$.
Thus by [[#^a413fa|Proposition]] 6.2.3, $F$ is a map of degree $p$.

> [!bluebox]+ **Proposition 6.2.6**: (Hartshorn IV 2.5)
> Let $f: X\to Y$ be a finite morphism of curves, and suppose that $K(X)$ is a purely inseparable extension of $K(Y)$. Then $X$ and $Y$ are isomorphic as abstract schemes, and $f$ is a composition of $k$-linear Frobenius morphisms. In particular $g(X) = g(Y)$
