### 6.1) The Hurwitz Theorem of Separable Morphisms

#### Definitions

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 6.1.1**: (Separable Morphism)
> Let $f: X\to Y$ be a finite morphism of non-singular curves. Then $f$ is said to be separable, if $K(X)$ is a separable extension of $K(Y)$

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 6.1.2**: (Ramification index)
> Let $f: X\to Y$ be a finite morphism of non-singular curves. Let $P$ be point in $X$. The the ramification index $e_P$ is defined to be coefficient of $P$ in the divisor $f^*(f(P))$.
> \
> More explicitly, if $t$ is a local parameter at $f(P)$, then $f$ induces a local map of local rings $f^*: \SO_{Y, f(P)} \to \SO_{X,P}$ and $e_P = \nu_P(f(t))$
> \
> We say $f$ is _ramified_ at $P$ if $e_P > 1$
> \
> Further, we say the ramification at $P$ is tame if $\textnormal {char } k = 0$ or if $\textnormal {char }k = p >0$ and $p$ does not not divide $e_p$. Otherwise we say the ramification at $P$ is wild.

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 6.1.3**: (Unramified morphism)
> A morphism of nonsingular curves $f: X\to Y$ is said to be unramified if $e_P = 1$ for all $P\in X$

Now we see what happens to differentials under this map.

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 6.1.4**: ($dt/du$)
> Let $f: X\to Y$ be a finite morphism of nonsingular curves, $P\in X$ and $Q = f(P)$.\
> Let $t$ be a local parameter at $Q$ and $u$ be a local parameter at $P$. $f$ induces $f^*: \SO_{Y,y} \to \OXx$, which then induces $\tilde f^*: \Omega_{\SO_{Y,y}/k}\to \Omega_{\OXx/k}$. Then $\tilde f^*(dt) = gdu$. Define $g = dt/du$

^1a45c2

> [!greenbox]+ **Lemma 6.1.5**:
> With notation as [[#^1a45c2|above]], If $t, t'$ are local parameters at $Q$ and $u, u'$ local parameters at $P$, then $dt/du$ and $dt'/du'$ differ only by units in $\OXx$ and $\nu_P(dt'/du') = \nu_P(dt/du)$. Thus this number is independent of the choice of local parameter.

^633cae

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> $dt$ and $dt'$ are both generators for $\Omega_{\SO_{Y,y}/k}$, which is free $\SO_{Y,y}$-module. Hence $dt' = adt$ where $a$ is a unit. Similarly, $du' = bdu$ where $b$ is a unit.
> \
> Thus $dt' = adt \mapsto f^*(a) (\frac {dt} {du}) du = b\inv f^*(a) \frac {dt} {du} du'$.
> And so, $dt'/du' = b\inv f^*(a) dt/du$. $b\inv f^*(a)$ is unit and hence we are done.

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 6.1.6**: ($R_P$)
> Let $f: X\to Y$ be a finite separable morphism of non-singular curves. Suppose $f(P) = Q$, $t$ a local parameter at $Q$ and $u$ a local parameter at $P$. Then $R_P$ is defined to be $\nu_P(dt/du)$.
> \
> From [[#^633cae|lemma]] 6.1.5 we that $R_P$ is well-defined and depend only on the point and the map, not on the choice of local parameters.

#### The Theorem

The Hurwitz Theorem allows us to measure _how much_ ramification is happening under a map. We will proceed to prove it analyzing some properties of the sheaf of differentials $\Omega_X$,$\Omega_Y$ and the sheaf of relative differentials $\Omega_{X/Y}$

> [!bluebox]+ **Proposition 6.1.7**: (Hartshorne IV 2.1)
> Let $f: X\to Y$ be a finite separable morphism of non-singular curves. Then sequence of [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/5) Sheaf of Differentials/5.2) Properties of Sheaf of Differentials#^6dc98b|Proposition]] 5.2.2 is exact on the left side as well, i.e. we get an exact sequence
>
> $$
> 0 \to f^*\Omega_Y \to \Omega_X \to \Omega_{X/Y} \to 0
> $$

^b7f49f

An important thing to note from the proof of the proposition above is that at the generic point $\eta$, $(\Omega_{X/Y})_\eta = 0$ because $f$ is a separable morphism.

> [!greenbox]+ **Proposition 6.1.8**: (Hartshorne IV 2.2 (b))
> Let $f: X\to Y$ be a finite separable morphism of non-singular curves and $P$ be a closed point. Then $\Omega_{X/Y}$ is a principal $\SO_P$ module of finite length, $\textnormal {length}(\Omega_{X/Y})_P = R_P$

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> Let $t, u$ be local parameters at $f(P)$ and $P$. Then we take stalks at $P$ of the exact sequence of [[#^b7f49f|Proposition]] 6.1.7. Observe that $(\Omega_{X})_P$ is free $\SO_P$ module generated by $du$ and $(f^*\Omega_Y)_P$ is free $\SO_P$ module generated by $1\otimes dt$
> \
> So, the cokernel of the map would be $\SO_P/ \ (\frac {dt} {du}) \SO_P$ which is a finite length $\SO_P$ module of length equal to $\nu_P(\frac {dt} {du}) = R_P$

> [!greenbox]+ **Proposition 6.1.9**: (Hartshorne IV 2.2 (c))
> Let $f : X\to Y$ be a finite separable morphism of non-singular curves. Then
>
> $$
> \textnormal{lenght}(\Omega_{X/Y}) = R_P \geq e_P - 1
> $$
>
> with equality if and only if the ramification is tame.

^37cdb6

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> Let $u$ be a local parameter at $P$ and $t$ a local parameter at $f(P)$.
> Suppose that under the map of local rings, $t\mapsto au^{e_P}$.
> So, $dt \mapsto e_Pu^{e_P-1}du + u^{e_P}da$ and since $du$ generates $\Omega_{\SO_P}$, $da$ must be of the form $bdu$ for some $b\in \SO_P$.
> \
> Putting this all together, we get $dt/du = u^{e_P-1}(e_P + bu)$
> We know that $dt/du \neq 0$ because $1\otimes dt$ is a generator for $(f^*\Omega_Y)_P$ and it maps to $(\frac {dt}{du})du$ which is non-zero because [[#^b7f49f|Proposition]] 6.1.7 tells us the map is injective.
> \
> Hence $R_P = \nu_P(\frac {dt}{du}) \geq e_P - 1$.
> Further $R_P = e_P -1 \iff e_P + bu$ is a unit $\iff$ $e_P$ is a unit $\iff$ $e_P \neq 0$ in $k$ $\iff$ the ramification is tame.

> [!greenbox]+ **Proposition 6.1.10**: (Hartshorne IV 2.2 (a))
> Let $f: X\to Y$ be a finite separable morphism of non-singular curves. Then $\Omega_{X/Y}$ is supported only at the points of ramification of $f$.
> \
> Further, $\Omega_{X/Y}$ is a torsion sheaf supported at finitely points and $f$ has finitely many points of ramification.

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> If $f$ is unramified at $P$, the $e_P =1$ and it is clearly tame irrespective of the characteristic of $k$, so $R_P = 0$. Then $(\Omega_{X/Y})_P = 0$.
> \
> Conversely, if $f$ is ramified at $P$, then $e_P \geq 2$, so $R_P \geq 1$. So $(\Omega_{X/Y})_P \neq 0$
> \
> To see that $\Omega_{X/Y}$ is a torsion sheaf, we can look at a cover where both $f^*\Omega_Y$ and $\Omega_X$ are free of rank $1$ and since the map $f^*\Omega_Y \to \Omega_X$ is injective, we get that their cokernel must be torsion.
> \
> If $\eta$ is the generic point of $X$, then $(\Omega_{X/Y})_{\eta}) = 0$, so $\eta$ is not in the support of $\Omega_{X/Y}$
> But $\Omega_{X/Y}$ is a coherent sheaf, being the cokernel of two coherent sheaf.
> And the support of a coherent sheaf on a Noetherian scheme is closed.
> Hence $\Omega_{X/Y}$ is supported at only finitely many points and thus $f$ has only finitely many points of ramification.

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 6.1.11**: (Ramification divisor)
> The ramification divisor is defined as
>
> $$
> R = \sum_{P\in X} R_P = \sum_{P\in X} (\Omega_{X/Y})_P
> $$

> [!greenbox]+ **Proposition 6.1.12**: (Hartshorne IV 2.3)
> Let $f: X\to Y$ be a finite separable morphism of non-singular curves and $K_X, K_Y$ canonical divisor on $X,Y$ respectively. Then
>
> $$
> K_X \sim f^*K_Y + R
> $$

^39fff1

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> We have the following exact sequence of sheaves of $\SO_X$-modules from [[#^b7f49f|Proposition]] 6.1.7
>
> $$
> 0 \to f^*\Omega_Y \to \Omega_X \to \Omega_{X/Y} \to 0
> $$
>
> Applying $\_\otimes_{\OX}\Omega_X\inv$ to the sequence above, we get
>
> $$
> 0 \to f^*\Omega_Y\otimes\Omega_X\inv \to \SO_X \to \Omega_{X/Y}\otimes\Omega_X\inv \to 0
> $$
>
> But $\Omega_{X/Y} \simeq \bigoplus_{P\in X} (\Omega_{X/Y})_P \simeq \bigoplus_{P\in X}\SO_P/\mathfrak m_P^{R_P}$
> So, $\Omega_{X/Y}\otimes \Omega_{X}\inv \simeq \bigoplus_{P\in X}\SO_P /\mathfrak m_P^{R_P}$.
> \
> Note that we have the following exact sequence.
>
> $$
> 0 \to \SL(-R) \to \OX \to \bigoplus_{P\in X}\SO_P/\mathfrak m_P^{R_P}\to 0
> $$
>
> Now be [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/Appendix/Sheaf Theoretic Results#^f8f57b|Proposition]] SH.4, any surjective map from $\OX \to \bigoplus_{P\in X}\SO_P/\mathfrak m_P^{R_P}$ has the same kernel. Thus $f^*\Omega_Y \otimes \Omega_X\inv \simeq \SL(-R)$ and so their divisors are linearly equivalent.
> \
> Thus we get $f^*K_Y + R \sim K_X$

> [!greenbox]+ **Theorem 6.1.13**: (Hurwitz Theorem) (Hartshorne IV 2.4)
> Let $f: X \to Y$ be a finite separable morphism of curves. Then
>
> $$
> 2g(X) - 2 = (\deg f)(2g(Y)- 2) + \deg R
> $$
>
> Further, if $f$ only has tame ramification, the $\deg R = \sum_{P\in X} (e_P - 1)$

> [!proof] _Proof_:
> This obtained by taking degrees of the linear equivalence from [[#^39fff1|Proposition]] 6.1.12.
> The second part of the theorem follows directly from [[#^37cdb6|Proposition]] 6.1.9.
