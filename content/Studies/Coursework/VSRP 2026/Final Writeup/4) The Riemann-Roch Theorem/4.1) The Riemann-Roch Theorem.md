### 4.1) The Riemann Roch Theorem

#### The Theorem

For the entirety of this section, $X$ is a nonsingular projective curve.

> [!yellowbox]+ Notation: For a divisor $D$ on $X$, by $l(D)$ denote $\Dims k \Gamma(X, \SL(D))$

On such an $X$, the canonical bundle $\omega_X = \Omega_{X/k}$ is a line bundle, from [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/5) Sheaf of Differentials/5.2) Properties of Sheaf of Differentials#^b82e06|Corollary]] 5.2.8. Let $K$ be any divisor such that $\omega_X \simeq \SL(K)$.

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 4.1.1**: (Geometric Genus $p_g$)
> Geometric genus is defined as $p_g = \Dims k \Gamma(X,\omega_X)$

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 4.1.2**: (Arithmetic Genus $p_a$)
> Let $X$ be a variety of dimension $r$, then arithmetic genus is defined as
> $p_a = (-1)^r(\chi(\OX) -1)$

> [!greenbox]+ **Lemma 4.1.3**: (Hartshorne IV 1.1)
> If $X$ is a curve, then
>
> $$
> p_a(X) = p_g(X) = \Dims k H^1(X,\OX)
> $$
>
> So we call this number simply the genus of $X$ and denote by $g$

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> Since $X$ is a curve, hence dimension $1$, hence by [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/2) Sheaf Cohomological Results/2) Sheaf Cohomological Results#^720302|Theorem]] 2.2, $\chi(\OX) = h^0(X,\OX) - h^1(X,\OX)$
> Since $X$ is projective, $H^0(X,\OX)= \Gamma(X,\OX) = k$, thus  $h^0(X,\OX) = 1$
> From this we get $p_a(X) = h^1(X,\OX)$
> \
> For $p_g$, we use [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/2) Sheaf Cohomological Results/2) Sheaf Cohomological Results#^634e0d|Serre Duality]].
> $H^1(X,\OX) \simeq H^0(X,\OX^\vee\otimes \omega_X) \simeq H^0(X,\omega_X)$
> Thus $h^1(X,\OX) = p_g$

> [!greenbox]+ **Lemma 4.1.4**: (Hartshorne IV 1.2)
> Let $D$ be divisor on a complete nonsingular curve $X$.
> $l(D) > 0 \implies \deg D \geq 0$
> Further, $l(D) > 0$ and $\deg D = 0 \implies D \sim 0$

^cf761d

> [!proof]- _Proof:_
> From [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/3) Divisors on Curves/3) Divisors on Curves#^7453ae|lemma]] 3.7 we get that $\deg D$ only depends on the linear equivalence class.
> Since $l(D) >0$, the linear system $|D|$ is non-empty and hence $D$ is linearly equivalent to an effective divisors. Thus $\deg D \geq 0$
> \
> For the second part, we know from the previous statement that $D$ is equivalent to an effective divisor. But the only effective divisor of degree $0$ is $0$. Thus $D\sim 0$, i.e. $D$ is principal.

> [!greenbox]+ **Theorem 4.1.5**: (The Riemann-Roch Theorem, Hartshorne IV 1.3)
> Let $X$ be a complete non-singular curve of genus $g$ and $D$ a divisor on it. Let $K$ be a canonical Divisor. Then we have:
>
> $$
> l(D) - l(K-D) = \deg D+1-g
> $$

^1476d4

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> First we note that $\chi(\SL(D)) = l(D) - l(K-D)$
> Since $\chi(\SL(D)) = h^0(X,\SL(D)) - h^1(X,\SL(D))$
> By [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/2) Sheaf Cohomological Results/2) Sheaf Cohomological Results#^634e0d|Serre Duality]], $h^1(X,\SL(D)) = h^0(X, \SL(D)^\vee\otimes \omega_X) = h^0(X,\SL(K-D))$
> Thus $\chi(\SL(D)) = l(D) - l(K-D)$
> \
> So now, we have to prove that $\chi(\SL(D)) = \deg D +1-g$
> \
> We will prove this by induction on the divisors.
> More precisely, we will show that for any point $P\in X$ and divisor $D$,
> the formula holds $D$ $\iff$ it holds for $D-P$
> \
> Base case: $D = 0$.
> Here, $l(D) = 1$, $l(K-D)= l(K) = g$ and $\deg D = 0$ thus the formula holds.
> \
> Now let $D$ be any divisor and $P \in X$.
> We have the following exact sequence:
>
> $$
> 0 \to \SL(-P) \to \OX \to \kk(P) \to 0
> $$
>
> This is because $\SL(-P)$ is the sheaf of ideals for the closed subscheme we get from the divisor $P$.
> Tensoring this exact sequence by $\SL(D)$ we get
>
> $$
> 0\to \SL(D- P) \to \SL(D) \to \kk(P)\otimes \SL(D)\to 0
> $$
>
> $\SL(D)$ is locally free, hence flat. Thus the sequence remains exact. And $(\kk(P) \otimes \SL(D))_x \simeq \kk(P)_x\otimes \OXx \simeq \kk(P)_x$. Thus [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/Appendix/Sheaf Theoretic Results#^2f3027|Corollary]] SH.3  applies and we get $\kk(P)\otimes \SL(D)\simeq \kk(P)$
> \
> Now by [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/2) Sheaf Cohomological Results/2) Sheaf Cohomological Results#^970f48|Lemma]] 2.5, $\chi (\SL(D)) = \chi(\SL(D-P)) + \chi(\kk(P))$
> And by [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/2) Sheaf Cohomological Results/2) Sheaf Cohomological Results#^a50a53|Corollary]] 2.7, $\chi(\kk(P))= \Dims k(k) = 1$
> \
> $\chi(\SL(D)) = \deg D +1 -g \implies \chi(\SL(D-P)) = \deg (D -P) +1 - g$ and
> $\chi(\SL(D-P) = \deg (D-P) +1 -g \implies \chi(D) = (\deg D+1 -g$
> Because $\deg (D-P) = \deg D - 1$
> \
> Since we can from $0$ to any divisor by adding or substracting one point at a time, the formula holds for all divisors and thus the statement is proven.

#### Some applications

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 4.1.6**: (Special/Non-Special Divisors)
> A divisor $D$ on a complete non-singular curve $X$ is said to be special if $L(K -D) > 0$. Otherwise it is called non-special

If a divisor is non-special, by Riemann-Roch theorem, we get $l(D) = \deg D +1 -g$

> [!greenbox]+ **Lemma 4.1.7**: (Degree of the Canonical divisor)
> Let $K$ be a divisor corresponding to $\omega_X$ on a complete nonsingular curve $X$ of genus $2$.
> Then $\deg K = 2g - 2$

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> Setting $D = K$ in the Riemann-Roch Theorem and noting that $l(K) = g$, we get $\deg K = 2g - 2$

> [!greenbox]+ **Lemma 4.1.8**:
> Let $D$ be divisor of positive degree on a complete non-singular curve $X$, then for large $n$,
> $\Dim |nD| = n\cdot\deg D -g$

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> For all $n$ such that $\deg(K - nD) < 0$, $l(K-D) = 0$. Thus the divisor is non-special and the result follows since $\Dim |nD| = l(D) - 1$

> [!greenbox]+ **Proposition 4.1.9**:
> Let $X$ be complete nonsingular curve of genus $g$
> Then $X$ is rational, i.e., $X\simeq \PP^1 \iff g = 0$

^b88ce6

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> First suppose $X\simeq \PP^1$.
> The line bundles $\SO_{\PP^1}(n) \simeq \SO_{\PP^1}(1)^{\otimes n}$. $\SO_{\PP^1}(n)$ is a degree $n$ line bundle and $l(\SO_{\PP^1}(n)) = n+1$
> For large $n$ these will be non-special and hence by Riemann-Roch, we get
> $n+1 = n +1 -g \implies g =0$
> \
> Now for the converse.
> Since $g = 0$, $\deg K= -2$
> For any two distinct points $P,Q\in X$, consider the divisor $D = P-Q$.
> Since $\deg D = 0, \deg D - K < 0$ and thus it is a non-special divisor.
> $l(D) = 1$ from Riemann Roch. But from [[#^cf761d|lemma]] 4.1.4, we must have $D \sim 0$ and so $P \sim  Q$
> So, from [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/3) Divisors on Curves/3) Divisors on Curves#^71aa25|lemma]] 3.8, $X\simeq \PP^1$

#### Group Structure on Elliptic curves

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 4.1.10**: (Elliptic Curve)
> An Elliptic Curve is a non-singular curve of genus $1$

Observe that on an elliptic curve, $\deg K = 0$ and $l(K) = 1$ thus by [[#^cf761d|lemma]] 4.1.4, $K \sim 0$ and $\omega_X \simeq \OX$.

As a consequence of this, from Riemann-Roch Theorem, we get
$l(D) - l(-D) = \deg D$.
If $\deg D > 0$, we get $l(D) = \deg D$ since $l(-D) = 0$
But we get learn very little about degree $0$ divisors.

> [!yellowbox]+ **Definition 4.1.11**: ($\PicO X$)
> For a complete non-singular curve $X$, $\Cl X\simeq \Pic X$ from [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/1) Setup/1.3) Invertible Sheaves#^6085e5|Propsition]] 1.3.9 and [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/1) Setup/1.2) Cartier Divisors#^a2f5ac|Proposition]] 1.2.10. And thus [[Studies/Coursework/VSRP 2026/Final Writeup/3) Divisors on Curves/3) Divisors on Curves#^7453ae|Lemma]] 3.7, every element of $\Pic X$ has a well-defined degree. Let $\PicO X$ be the degree zero elements of $\Pic X$

> [!greenbox]+ **Proposition 4.1.12**: (Correspondence between point of Elliptic curves and $\PicO X$)
> Fix a point $P_0\in X$. Then $P \mapsto \SL(P-P_0)$ gives a one-to-one correspondence between point of $X$ and $\PicO X$

> [!proof]- _Proof_:
> We can prove this considering the mapping $X\to \Cl X$ with $P \mapsto P - P_0$.
> \
> Let $D$ be degree $0$ divisor. Consider $D+P_0$
> This is a divisor of positive degree, thus $l(D+P_0) = 1 \implies \Dim|D+P| = 0$
> So, there is a unique effective divisor such that $D+P_0\sim D'$.
> But $D'$ is an effective divisor of degree $1$, thus it must corresponds to a point.
> Thus there is a unique point $P$ such that $D\sim P-P_0$

This lets us transfer the group structure on $\PicO X$ to closed points of $X$ with $P_0$ as the identity element.
